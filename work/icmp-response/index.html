02 / Framework mapping

Five course functions.
One current extension.

The supplied teaching template uses the older five-function structure. The original report follows that assignment. CSF 2.0 adds Govern as a sixth function; the governance entry below is a portfolio extension, not part of the original submitted response.

ID / Identify

Understand the exposure

Inventory affected assets and access privileges; review the firewall gap and availability risk.

PR / Protect

Reduce the attack surface

Maintain firewall policy, apply selective ICMP rate limits and validate source addresses.

DE / Detect

Recognize abnormal traffic

Baseline network behavior, configure monitoring and triage IDS/IPS alerts.

RS / Respond

Contain and communicate

Apply emergency containment, preserve evidence and coordinate incident decisions.

RC / Recover

Restore and verify

Restore critical services first, verify availability and update the recovery process.

GV / Govern · CSF 2.0 extension

Assign decisions and ownership

Name policy owners, set risk priorities, define escalation and review supplier support and lessons learned.

03 / Sustainable improvement

Control the flood.
Keep useful ICMP working.

Blocking incoming ICMP was the scenario’s emergency containment measure. A lasting policy should distinguish message types, direction and necessary network functions. ICMP carries useful diagnostics and error signals; IPv6 also relies on ICMPv6 for essential functions. Blanket blocking can create connectivity problems.

  1. 01Review and tune

    Document required ICMP messages and test rate limits and firewall rules against legitimate connectivity. Validate source-address rules and IDS/IPS behavior.

  2. 02Validate restoration

    Confirm critical resources are reachable, monitor error rates and traffic behavior, then restore remaining services with recorded checks.

  3. 03Practice and improve

    Exercise the response plan, review alert quality and recovery dependencies, and assign owners and review dates for corrective actions.

These proposed validation steps extend the supplied analysis. No live network changes, recovery test or future outage reduction was measured in this activity.

Incident report analysis

Instructions

As you continue through this course, you may use this template to record your findings after completing an activity or to take notes on what you've learned about a specific tool or concept. You can also use this chart as a way to practice applying the NIST framework to different situations you encounter.

Summary

The organization experienced a Denial-of-Service (DoS) attack that disrupted its internal network for two hours. A malicious actor flooded the network with ICMP packets through an improperly configured firewall, overwhelming network services and preventing employees from accessing resources.

Identify

The organization's internal network services were unavailable for two hours due to a flood of ICMP packets from a malicious actor. The investigation identified an improperly configured firewall as the main vulnerability. Only the internal network traffic was affected, and they could not access any network resources. Coworkers needed access to the internal networks to continue their daily business.

Protect

To prevent events like this from repeating in the future, the network security team implements the following:
  • A new firewall rule to limit the rate of incoming ICMP packets.
  • Source IP address verification on the firewall to check for spoofed IP addresses on incoming ICMP packets.
  • Network monitoring software to detect abnormal traffic patterns.
  • An IDS/IPS system to filter out some ICMP traffic based on suspicious characteristics.

Regular firewall maintenance should be conducted to prevent similar attacks in the future.

Detect

Network monitoring software was implemented to identify abnormal traffic patterns and potential attacks. The IDS/IPS system can detect suspicious ICMP traffic and alert the security team. Continuous monitoring will help identify threats earlier and reduce response times.

Respond

The incident management team blocked incoming ICMP packets, temporarily shut down non-critical network services, and prioritized restoring critical services. The security team investigated the attack and identified the firewall vulnerability. Incident response procedures should be reviewed and improved to minimize future disruptions.

Recover

The organization restored critical network services after the attack was contained. The security team should verify that all affected services are functioning properly before restoring non-critical services. Regular testing and improved recovery procedures can help reduce downtime during future incidents.

Reflections/Notes:

This incident demonstrates the importance of properly configured firewalls, continuous network monitoring, and effective incident response procedures. Applying the NIST CSF can help the organization strengthen its network security and minimize the impact of future attacks.

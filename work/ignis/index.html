  1. Validate before display

    The FIRMS parser checks CSV headers, coordinates, finite values and UTC timestamps. Malformed rows cause an explicit error rather than an apparently complete feed.

    Inspect CSV validation

  2. Preserve source and state

    The collector records acquisition/retrieval times and source status, gives observations stable IDs and queues batches for authenticated upload. The local outbox retains failed deliveries for retry.

    Inspect collection & retry

  3. Filter at the read boundary

    The public observations endpoint accepts 1, 6, 12 or 24 hours and known sources. It reports source availability and truncation separately from the result list. Only published incidents and approved perimeters reach public readers.

01 / The challenge

MAKE THE DATA
MAKE SENSE.

A list of coordinates does not tell much of a story on its own. The goal was to make satellite heat observations easier to understand through a map, useful time filters, and a readable observation list.

Behind that interface, the data needs a consistent structure. Fetching the observations, handling timestamps, and filtering results are all part of making the experience dependable.

02 / The process

START SMALL.
THEN GO DEEPER.

The first approach used Streamlit and Folium to explore the idea. Loading and interaction concerns then led toward Flask and a simpler frontend.

That transition brought more control over the interface. The backend stays focused on requesting, parsing, and filtering observations, while the frontend gives people a clear way to explore them.

03 / The decisions

CLARITY IS
A FEATURE.

The map and observation list sit close together, connecting a point with its details. Time filters for 1, 6, 12, and 24 hours narrow the view.

Straightforward German copy keeps the interface approachable. The user should be able to explore the information without learning the vocabulary of the underlying API.

04 / Where it stands

A PROTOTYPE.
A LOT LEARNED.

Ignis currently focuses on exploring satellite heat observations. The public pilot now adds reviewed mapped perimeters and weather context; the repository documents those workflows and their limitations. Operational alerting remains subject to validation.

The image above shows an earlier application interface. The linked demo opens the current public version. Satellite heat detections require interpretation and do not automatically confirm a wildfire.

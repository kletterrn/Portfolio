01 / The challenge

GIVE ATTENTION
A DIRECTION.

Controls, camera movement, and HUD information compete for a player’s attention. The challenge is to make the current state understandable while keeping interaction consistent.

That means thinking beyond where a label sits. The relationship between an input, the response, and the feedback is part of the interface.

02 / The approach

ONE INTERACTION.
THEN THE NEXT.

Larger systems become easier to reason about when they are broken into smaller behaviors. I use that approach to work through controls and the visual states around them.

The Orion aircraft model brings that work into three dimensions. The interactive showcase above uses the geometry from my Blender file, including the airframe, landing gear, and smaller surface details.

03 / The design question

WHAT DOES THE
PLAYER NEED NOW?

My current focus is information hierarchy, camera interaction, and clear visual feedback. Essential information should remain readable without taking over the scene.

The model viewer lets you inspect the aircraft from different angles. The presentation uses simplified studio materials for the web; the mod itself remains in development.

Move the camera, keep the subject.

Sensor movement should adjust stabilized framing without dropping the observed world point. The control help separates camera adjustment, recentering, optical field of view, and digital crop, rather than presenting them as one generic zoom.

Leave the controls, keep the task.

Normal operator exit and actual link loss mean different things. The documented design keeps the assigned automatic task on exit, resumes it after temporary manual takeover, and reserves return-home behavior for a lost link.

These are behaviors documented in the project source and R25 validation notes. The browser viewer is an aircraft asset study, not a simulation of those systems. Its geometry comes from an earlier Blender asset revision; it is not proof of the current runtime package.

04 / Where it stands

STILL BUILDING.
STILL LEARNING.

Orion remains in development. This case study describes the direction and the interaction problems I’m exploring, rather than a completed feature list.

The repository records local native compilation, packaging, and specific runtime fixtures for its R25 revision. Dedicated-server testing with separate clients, ordinary physical keyboard/mouse acceptance, cross-resolution visual checks, listening/flyby captures, and endurance testing remain outstanding. Those records are not independent validation by this portfolio review.
